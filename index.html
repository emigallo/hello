Systems I designed and kept alive.

Three diagrams and notes on a business platform I evolved as end-to-end technical lead over the past few years.

The material is organized from the outside in: first the deployment ecosystem, then the internal architecture of the business core, and finally the continuous delivery process that supports the system's evolution.

01 — Deployment view

The platform's physical ecosystem.

Four delivery channels coexist over a single business logic core: a mobile app for field logistics, a desktop client for administration, a REST API for external integrations, and batch processes for scheduled synchronization jobs.

Convergence on the shared business core guarantees functional consistency across channels without duplicating logic. The integration with the external ERP is encapsulated in a dedicated service, which isolates the rest of the system from the legacy's technical constraints.

02 — Internal architecture

Inside the shared core.

The core that appears as a black box in the previous diagram follows Clean Architecture principles, with strict layer separation and dependencies pointing toward the domain.

The Use cases layer orchestrates the interaction between the outside world and the domain. The Domain layer holds the business rules and knows nothing about the database or the frameworks. Cross-cutting groups the transversal utilities used across inner layers. External adapters are only reached through Ports defined by the domain.

03 — Delivery pipeline

The process that sustains evolution.

The pipeline follows the continuous delivery approach. Every change goes through three stages before reaching production.

The Commit stage runs fast validations on every push. The Acceptance stage builds the different artifacts in parallel, runs acceptance and integration tests, and deploys to QA. The transition to production is done by manual promotion of the same already-validated artifact — nothing gets recompiled.

Measurable outcome
We went from one deployment per week to several per week, with measurable stability on each release.
